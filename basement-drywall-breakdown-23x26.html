Step-by-Step Construction Sequence

Step 1
Foam Barrier & Moisture Seal
Glue 25 sheets of 1.5″ FOAMULAR XPS directly to concrete with PL 300 adhesive. Seal every vertical and horizontal seam tightly with 3M 8067 / Tuck Tape.
Step 2
Snap Lines, Sill Gasket & Plates
Snap chalk lines 4″ off foam. Lay closed-cell sill gasket under pressure-treated bottom plates and anchor into slab with Tapcon screws every 24″–32″. Fasten SPF top plate to joists above.
Step 3
Plumb Studs & Code Fireblocking
Toe-nail 88 studs at 16″ on-center (measure each board; floors slope). Install 2×4 horizontal fireblocking every 10′ and seal cavity tops to ceiling joists with orange firebreak foam (IRC R302.11).
Step 4
Drywall Ceiling, Walls & Finish
Hang 21 ceiling panels first using panel hoist. Hang 27 wall panels horizontally, holding 1/2″ above concrete slab. Finish with 3 coats (Easy Sand tape coat + Plus 3 skims) and PVA primer.

Kansas City Climate Zone 4A/5A Technical & Code Notes

1. Condensation & Dew Point In Kansas City, underground soil stays at ~52°F year-round. Summer basement air at 70°F with 65% RH has a dew point of 57°F. Without continuous 1.5″+ XPS rigid foam, water vapor permeates cavities and condenses into liquid against bare concrete, creating severe hidden mold.
2. Beam & Soffit Framing A 23′ × 26′ span exceeds standard 2×10 / 2×12 lumber capabilities. Your room will feature a central steel I-beam or LVL girder with 1–2 steel lally columns. Plan to frame 2×2 or 2×4 ladder soffits around that girder and main HVAC duct trunks before hanging ceiling board (8 corner beads included).
3. Turnkey Economics A Kansas City framing and drywall sub typically charges $12–$15/LF for framing and $2.75–$3.50/sq ft for drywall hanging and Level 4 finish ($5,500 – $7,200 turnkey). Doing it yourself with bulk materials ($2,802) plus tool rentals ($200) yields over $3,500 in savings.
